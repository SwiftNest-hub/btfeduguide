Study Abroad

Study abroad, step by step

Short, original explanations you can read in any order.

What studying abroad involves

Studying abroad means completing part or all of a qualification in another country. It involves choosing a programme, meeting admission rules, funding your studies, getting permission to enter and study, and settling in. Start early: many steps depend on documents that take time to obtain.

How to choose a destination

Compare language of instruction, relative cost, programme availability, funding routes and rules on student work. Use the comparison tool on the Destinations page, then confirm details on official sources.

Researching universities and colleges

Check that the institution is recognised by the country's education authority, read the programme page for entry requirements, and ask what support international students receive.

Undergraduate and postgraduate programmes

Undergraduate programmes (usually bachelor's degrees) follow secondary school. Postgraduate programmes (master's, doctorates) follow a first degree. Lengths and structures differ by country.

Admission requirements

Typical requests include academic records, identification, a personal statement, references and sometimes tests or interviews. Each institution sets its own list. Always follow the programme page.

Academic qualifications and transcripts

Request official transcripts early. Some institutions want documents sent directly from your school, and some require certified translation or credential evaluation.

English-language requirements

Where English is the language of instruction, you may be asked for a test result or evidence of prior English-medium study. Which tests and scores are accepted varies by institution.

Application timelines and intakes

Universities admit students in intakes, commonly autumn and sometimes spring or winter. Deadlines differ by programme, so work backwards from the earliest date and leave time for documents and visa processing.

Tuition and living expenses

Budget for tuition, accommodation, food, transport, health cover and travel. Costs vary widely by country and city, so use each institution's published figures instead of averages.

Scholarships and funding

Funding comes from governments, institutions and foundations. Read eligibility and deadlines on the official provider page. See our scholarship directory.

Accommodation and student life

Ask the university about on-campus housing, private rentals and how to avoid rental scams. Look for orientation programmes and student support services.

Common application mistakes

Missing deadlines, submitting unofficial documents, copying generic personal statements, ignoring programme-specific requirements, and trusting anyone who guarantees admission.

Preparing before departure

Confirm your permission to study, arrange funds and insurance, keep copies of key documents, learn about local arrival steps, and make a plan for the first month.
